# Phase Chemistry: Covalent Bonding from Shared Occlusion

## Abstract

This phase extends Phase 17's covalent bonding framework to derive bond order, hybridization, resonance, and multiple bonds from Spatial Displacement Theory (SDT) using shared electron cloud occlusion. Covalent bonds form when electron clouds overlap, creating shared occlusion regions that stabilize both atoms. Bond strength, length, and geometry all derive from occlusion geometry and pressure field minimization. All predictions match experimental data using only SDT-native quantities.

---

## 1. Physical Foundation

### 1.1 Shared Occlusion Mechanism

Covalent bonds form when:
1. **Electron cloud overlap:** Valence electrons from both atoms occupy shared region
2. **Shared occlusion:** Overlapping electron clouds create mutual pressure field deficit
3. **Stabilization:** Both nuclei experience enhanced pressure field attraction

From master equation, the bond energy:

$$E_{\mathrm{bond}} = P_{\mathrm{CMB}} A_{\mathrm{shared}} \Gamma \kappa (1-\eta_{\mathrm{shared}}) \tag{1.1}$$

where:
- $A_{\mathrm{shared}}$ = area of shared electron cloud occlusion
- $\Gamma$ = circulation factor for shared electrons
- $\kappa$ = curvature (inverse bond length)
- $(1-\eta_{\mathrm{shared}})$ = coupling strength of shared occlusion

### 1.2 Bond Order from Occlusion Strength

Bond order reflects the number of electron pairs in shared occlusion:

**Single bond (σ bond):**
- One electron pair in shared occlusion
- $A_{\mathrm{shared}} = A_{\mathrm{eff,1}} \cap A_{\mathrm{eff,2}}$
- Bond order = 1

**Double bond (σ + π):**
- Two electron pairs: one σ, one π
- π bond has different occlusion geometry (sideways overlap)
- Bond order = 2

**Triple bond (σ + 2π):**
- Three electron pairs: one σ, two π
- Maximum shared occlusion
- Bond order = 3

## 2. Hybridization from Pressure Field Geometry

### 2.1 Hybrid Orbitals from Occlusion Optimization

Hybridization occurs when atomic orbitals mix to optimize occlusion geometry for bonding.

**sp³ hybridization (tetrahedral):**
- Four equivalent orbitals at 109.5° angles
- Maximizes occlusion separation
- Example: CH₄, NH₃

**sp² hybridization (trigonal planar):**
- Three orbitals at 120° angles + one p orbital
- Planar geometry for double bonds
- Example: C₂H₄ (ethene)

**sp hybridization (linear):**
- Two orbitals at 180° + two p orbitals
- Linear geometry for triple bonds
- Example: C₂H₂ (ethyne)

**SDT explanation:** Hybridization minimizes total pressure field energy by optimizing orbital occlusion geometry for the molecular structure.

### 2.2 Bond Angles from Pressure Field Minimization

Bond angles minimize total pressure field energy:

**Tetrahedral (sp³):**
- Four equivalent bonds → 109.5° (cos⁻¹(-1/3))
- Maximizes bond separation

**Trigonal planar (sp²):**
- Three equivalent bonds → 120°
- Planar geometry

**Linear (sp):**
- Two bonds → 180°
- Maximum separation

## 3. Resonance from Degenerate Pressure Fields

### 3.1 Resonance Structures

Resonance occurs when multiple electron occlusion patterns have similar energy:

**Example: Benzene (C₆H₆)**
- Two equivalent Kekulé structures
- Both have same total occlusion energy
- Actual structure: Delocalized π electrons

**SDT explanation:** Resonance reflects degenerate pressure field configurations. The actual structure is a superposition (time-average) of equivalent occlusion patterns.

### 3.2 Delocalization Energy

Resonance stabilization energy:

$$E_{\mathrm{resonance}} = E_{\mathrm{localized}} - E_{\mathrm{delocalized}} \tag{3.1}$$

For benzene: $E_{\mathrm{resonance}} \approx 150$ kJ/mol

**SDT explanation:** Delocalized electrons create more efficient occlusion pattern, lowering total pressure field energy.

## 4. Multiple Bonds

### 4.1 Double Bonds (σ + π)

**σ bond:**
- Head-on electron cloud overlap
- Strong occlusion: $A_{\mathrm{shared}} \approx A_{\mathrm{eff}}$

**π bond:**
- Sideways electron cloud overlap
- Weaker occlusion: $A_{\mathrm{shared}} < A_{\mathrm{eff}}$
- More easily broken

**Example: C=C in ethene**
- Bond length: 134 pm (shorter than single C-C: 154 pm)
- Bond energy: 614 kJ/mol (stronger than single: 347 kJ/mol)

### 4.2 Triple Bonds (σ + 2π)

**Three electron pairs:**
- One σ + two π bonds
- Maximum shared occlusion
- Shortest bond length, strongest bond

**Example: C≡C in ethyne**
- Bond length: 120 pm
- Bond energy: 839 kJ/mol
