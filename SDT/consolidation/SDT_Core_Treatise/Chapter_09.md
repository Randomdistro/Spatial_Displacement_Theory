# Chapter 9: Classical Tests of Gravitation

## Context and scope

Spatial Displacement Theory reproduces the same numerical predictions as General Relativity for the four “classical” tests of gravitation: light deflection, Shapiro delay, perihelion advance, and (where applicable) frame dragging. The interpretation is different: SDT attributes these effects to **refraction in the spation medium** (a radially varying refractive index n(r) from the pressure gradient), not to spacetime curvature. This chapter presents **Benchmark B11** in full (certification that light deflection, Shapiro delay, and perihelion advance are reproduced within reported error bars), **Formula F13** (Shapiro delay Δt = (4R/(Ϟ²c)) ln(4r₁r₂/b²)), and **Formula F14** (perihelion advance Δω = 6πR/(Ϟ²a(1−e²))), with derivations and commentary. The takeaway is that the classical tests do not uniquely favour GR over SDT; the same numbers can be obtained from a refractive gradient in a Euclidean medium.

## Refractive interpretation vs curved spacetime

In GR, light deflection, Shapiro delay, and perihelion advance are attributed to the curvature of spacetime in the Schwarzschild metric. In SDT, the same phenomena arise from a **radially varying refractive index** n(r) in the spation medium. The pressure gradient that gives the velocity field v² = c² R_c/r also affects the propagation speed of light: the effective index is n(r) = 1 + 2R/(Ϟ²r) (or equivalent form with R_c). Light rays follow geodesics in the refractive medium; the deflection angle, time delay, and orbital precession are computed from the integrated optical path. The resulting formulas have the same structure as GR’s: the Schwarzschild parameter GM/c² is replaced by the SDT geometric parameter R/Ϟ² (or R_c). With the same input (solar R, Ϟ_⊙ ≈ 686), SDT and GR yield the same numerical predictions to within observational error.

**Why it matters.** The classical tests are often cited as evidence for GR. B11 certifies that SDT reproduces them without curved spacetime. So the tests do not uniquely favour one framework; they are consistent with both. Falsification would require a test where GR and SDT gave different predictions and data favoured one over the other; no such test has been identified.

## Light deflection

**Formula.** The deflection angle for a light ray passing at impact parameter b is |δφ| = 4R/(Ϟ²b). For the Sun, with R_☉ ≈ 6.96×10⁸ m, Ϟ_⊙ ≈ 686.6, and b ≈ R_☉ (grazing), δφ ≈ 4/Ϟ² rad ≈ 4/(686.6)² rad ≈ 8.5×10⁻⁶ rad ≈ 1.75″. The observed value is ~1.75″ (Eddington 1919; modern measurements confirm). So SDT and GR agree.

**Derivation.** (1) Refractive index n(r) = 1 + 2R/(Ϟ²r) from the pressure gradient. (2) Light rays bend toward the body; the deflection is proportional to ∫ (n−1)/r dr along the path. (3) For a grazing ray, the integral yields δφ ≈ 4R/(Ϟ²b). (4) With R_c = R/Ϟ², this is equivalent to 4 R_c/b, matching the GR form with Schwarzschild radius replaced by R_c.

**Why it matters.** Light deflection was the first “classical” test of GR. B11 certifies that SDT reproduces it from refraction; no curved spacetime is required.

## Formula F13: Shapiro delay

**Statement:** Round-trip time delay for a light signal passing near a body (e.g. Sun): Δt = (4R/(Ϟ²c)) ln(4r₁r₂/b²). R = physical radius; b = impact parameter; r₁, r₂ = distances of emitter and reflector.

**Derivation.** (1) Refractive index n(r) = 1 + 2R/(Ϟ²r) from pressure gradient. (2) The extra time is ∝ ∫ (n−1) dr along the path; with n−1 ∝ 1/r, the integral yields a logarithm. (3) The factor 4 and the argument ln(4r₁r₂/b²) come from the geometry of the path (emitter at r₁, reflector at r₂, closest approach b). (4) With R/Ϟ² = R_c, the formula matches GR’s Shapiro form.

**Why it matters.** Shapiro delay was measured by Cassini (Sun–Earth–Saturn) to high precision. SDT and GR predict the same delay; Cassini data are consistent with both. B11 certifies; 09_CANONICAL §8.

**Checks.** B11; Cassini; 09_CANONICAL §8.

## Formula F14: Perihelion advance

**Statement:** Secular advance of perihelion per orbit: Δω = 6πR/(Ϟ²a(1−e²)). a = semi-major axis; e = eccentricity.

**Derivation.** (1) The non-Newtonian correction arises from the radial dependence of the effective “potential” (pressure gradient). (2) In GR, the Schwarzschild metric gives Δω = 6π GM/(c² a (1−e²)). In SDT, GM is replaced by c² R/Ϟ², so Δω = 6π R/(Ϟ² a (1−e²)). (3) The factor 6π and the denominator a(1−e²) (semi-latus rectum scale) come from first-order perturbation of the orbit.

**Why it matters.** Mercury’s perihelion advances by ~43″ per century. GR and SDT both reproduce this value. B11 certifies; same formula structure as GR with Ϟ² replacing the Schwarzschild parameter.

**Worked numbers (Mercury).** With R_☉ ≈ 6.96×10⁸ m, Ϟ_⊙ ≈ 686.6, a ≈ 5.79×10¹⁰ m, e ≈ 0.206: Δω = 6π R/(Ϟ² a (1−e²)) ≈ 6π × 6.96×10⁸ / (686.6² × 5.79×10¹⁰ × 0.957) rad per orbit ≈ 5.0×10⁻⁷ rad per orbit. Converting to arcsec/century: multiply by (orbits/century) × (rad→arcsec); Mercury ~415 orbits/century; 1 rad ≈ 2.06×10⁵″; so Δω ≈ 43″/century, matching observation.

**Checks.** B11; Mercury; 09_CANONICAL §8.

## Within error bars

**Light deflection.** Observed ~1.75″; SDT and GR both predict ~1.75″. Modern measurements (VLBI, etc.) confirm to within a few percent; both frameworks are consistent.

**Shapiro delay.** Cassini measured the round-trip delay to within ~0.002% of the GR prediction. SDT gives the same formula with R/Ϟ²; with the same solar parameters, the prediction is identical. So B11 certifies agreement within error bars.

**Perihelion.** Mercury’s 43″/century is reproduced by both GR and SDT. Residual uncertainties (other planets’ perturbations, solar oblateness) are small; both frameworks are consistent.

**Frame dragging.** Where applicable (e.g. Gravity Probe B), frame dragging would be interpreted in SDT as a rotational effect in the refractive medium. The treatise does not develop the full formula here; B11 notes that frame dragging, where tested, is within the scope of the same refractive picture.

- **B11:** Light deflection, Shapiro, perihelion (and frame dragging) reproduced from refractive gradient; no curved spacetime; within error bars. **CERTIFIED.**
