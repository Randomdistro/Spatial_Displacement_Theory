## PART I: INTRODUCTION

### Section 1.1: The Nitrogen Cycle Context

Nitrogen is the most abundant element in Earth's atmosphere, making up 78% as diatomic N₂. The nitrogen cycle encompasses all transformations of nitrogen through biological, geological, and industrial processes. This volume analyzes nitrogen compounds through the lens of Spatial Displacement Theory, where all chemistry is driven by nuclear structure and nuclear forces.

**Nitrogen's Central Role:**
- Atmospheric N₂: 78% of atmosphere, triple-bonded, extremely stable
- Biological nitrogen: Amino acids, nucleic acids, proteins (life's building blocks)
- Industrial nitrogen: Fertilizers, explosives, materials
- Environmental nitrogen: NOx compounds, nitrates, atmospheric chemistry

**The Nitrogen Cycle:**
1. **Fixation:** N₂ → NH₃ (biological or industrial)
2. **Nitrogen assimilation:** NH₃ → biological molecules
3. **Nitrification:** NH₃ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ (bacterial oxidation)
4. **Denitrification:** NO₃⁻ → N₂ (bacterial reduction)

**SDT Principle:** All nitrogen chemistry is driven by the nuclear structure of nitrogen-14 (7 protons + 7 neutrons = 14 nucleons = Carbon-12 core + deuteron). The "Locked Gear" structure of N-14 creates its unique chemistry.
