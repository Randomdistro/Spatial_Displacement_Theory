occur.

## Chapter 02: CMB Cause of Gravitation (Journal Submission)

### Abstract

Gravitation is a pressure-gradient consequence of the CMB acting on a pressurized matrix. This chapter
derives the inverse-square acceleration without invoking a fundamental $G$ or fundamental mass.

### Pressure Field and Potential Parameter

The ambient pressure is

$$
\Pi_0 = K_{\text{bulk}} = 4.6 \times 10^{113}\ \text{Pa}.
$$

For a displaced volume, the far-field pressure is

$$
\Pi_s(r) = \Pi_0 - \frac{\beta \rho_s}{r},
$$

with

$$
\beta = \frac{\kappa V_{\text{total}} K_{\text{bulk}}}{4\pi \rho_s}
      = \frac{\kappa V_{\text{total}} c^2}{4\pi}.
$$

### Acceleration from Pressure Gradient

Differentiate the pressure field:

$$
\frac{d\Pi_s}{dr} = \frac{\beta \rho_s}{r^2}.
$$

Acceleration follows as

$$
a(r) = -\frac{1}{\rho_s}\frac{d\Pi_s}{dr} = -\frac{\beta}{r^2}.
$$

This is the inverse-square law expressed without a fundamental $G$.

### Screening and Force Hierarchy

Composite bodies screen their own displacement field:

$$
\xi = \frac{V_{\text{disp,eff}}}{V_{\text{disp,total}}} \approx 5.6 \times 10^{-9}.
$$

This screening produces the vast disparity between short-range and long-range coupling without
introducing extra physics.

### Relativistic Effects as Pressure Optics

A pressure gradient implies a refractive index gradient:

$$
n(r) = 1 - \frac{\beta}{c^2 r}.
$$

This produces light deflection and time dilation effects consistent with observed values.
