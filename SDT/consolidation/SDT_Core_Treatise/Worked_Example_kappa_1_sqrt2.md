# SDT convention and a worked example

## SDT convention: Ϟ (U+03DE) and ϟ (U+03DF) — at all scales, including nuclear

The **convention** in SDT is the same everywhere:

- **Ϟ (U+03DE)** = variable velocity-ratio = **c/v_surface** (at a given radius; at Bohr radius that’s Ϟ_H = 137.036; at nucleon surface it’s the nucleon surface value, e.g. √2).
- **ϟ (U+03DF)** = fixed value at the c-boundary; **ϟ = 1** (at r = r_c, orbital velocity = c).

So **v_surface = c/Ϟ** at every scale. At the c-boundary, **Ϟ = ϟ = 1**. The main equations are:

- **v = (c/Ϟ)√(R/r)** — master orbital equation (equivalently v = c√(r_c/r) with r_c = R/Ϟ²).
- **z · k² = 1** — redshift–displacement identity (k = Ϟ at surface).
- **Rotation route to Ϟ:** Ϟ = √(πc/v_rot); **v_rot = π v_orb²/c** (F8).

**Even in nuclear** the convention is **Ϟ and ϟ (U+03DE, U+03DF)**. At the nucleon surface, if the velocity is c/√2, then the surface value is **Ϟ = c/(c/√2) = √2** (so we still use Ϟ, not a different symbol). At the c-boundary we still have **ϟ = 1**. So: **everywhere it is Ϟ and ϟ.**

---

## Worked example: Ϟ with real-world numbers

**Hydrogen (Bohr radius):**  
Ϟ_H = c/v_electron ≈ **137.036**. v_surface (electron) = c/Ϟ_H ≈ 2.188×10⁶ m/s. r_c = R/Ϟ²; z·Ϟ² = 1.

**Sun:**  
Ϟ_⊙ ≈ 686.6. v_surface (effective) = c/Ϟ_⊙ ≈ 436.5 km/s. r_c(☉) ≈ 1.48 km. z·Ϟ² = 1.

**Nucleon (if surface velocity is c/√2):**  
Surface value **Ϟ = c/(c/√2) = √2 ≈ 1.414**. So at the nucleon we still use **Ϟ** (and at c-boundary **ϟ = 1**). Same convention: v = (c/Ϟ)√(R/r), z·Ϟ² = 1. No separate symbol; it’s **Ϟ and ϟ** in nuclear too.

---

**Summary:** **Ϟ (U+03DE) = c/v_surface; ϟ (U+03DF) = 1 at c-boundary. v = (c/Ϟ)√(R/r), z·k²=1, rotation Ϟ = √(πc/v_rot). This holds at all scales, including nuclear. Even in nuclear it is ϟ (U+03DF).**
