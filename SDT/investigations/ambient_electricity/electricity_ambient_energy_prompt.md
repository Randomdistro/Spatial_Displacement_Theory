## Part 11: Detailed Numerical Examples and Worked Calculations

### 11.1 Atmospheric Electric Field Harvesting - Complete Calculation

#### Example 1: Maximum Power from Atmospheric Field

**Given:**
- Atmospheric E-field: $E_{atm} = 130$ V/m (fair weather)
- Collector area: $A = 1$ m²
- Load resistance: $R_{load} = 10$ MΩ (matched to source impedance)

**Step 1: Calculate Open-Circuit Voltage**

For vertical collector of height $h = 1$ m:
$$V_{oc} = E_{atm} \times h = 130 \text{ V/m} \times 1 \text{ m} = 130 \text{ V}$$

**Step 2: Calculate Source Impedance**

Atmospheric field source impedance (capacitive):
$$Z_{source} = \frac{1}{j\omega C_{air}}$$

where $C_{air} = \varepsilon_0 A/d$ is the capacitance to ground.

For $A = 1$ m², $d = 1$ m (height above ground):
$$C_{air} = \frac{8.854 \times 10^{-12} \times 1}{1} = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F}$$

At DC (or very low frequency), impedance is very high (essentially open circuit).

**Step 3: Calculate Maximum Power**

For matched load ($R_{load} = R_{source}$):
$$P_{max} = \frac{V_{oc}^2}{4R_{load}}$$

However, for atmospheric field, the source is more like a current source than voltage source.

Better model: Power = Current × Voltage

Atmospheric current density: $J_{atm} \approx 2 \times 10^{-12}$ A/m²

Current: $I = J_{atm} \times A = 2 \times 10^{-12} \times 1 = 2 \times 10^{-12}$ A

Power: $P = I \times V = 2 \times 10^{-12} \times 130 = 2.6 \times 10^{-10}$ W = 0.26 nW

**Step 4: SDT Interpretation**

From SDT master equation:
$$\dot{E} = P_{CMB} A_{eff} \Gamma \kappa (1-\eta)$$

For atmospheric harvesting:
- $P_{CMB} = 2.036 \times 10^{-2}$ Pa
- $A_{eff} = 1$ m² (collector area)
- $\Gamma$: Circulation factor from atmospheric currents
- $\kappa$: Curvature from field geometry
- $(1-\eta)$: Traction efficiency

**Result:** Maximum extractable power ≈ 0.26 nW/m² (extremely small)

## Part 14: Computational Simulation Framework

### 14.1 Electric Field Solver Implementation

#### Algorithm 1: Finite Difference Poisson Solver

**Problem:** Solve $\nabla^2 \Phi = -\rho_q/\varepsilon_0$ in 3D

**Method:** Finite Difference Method (FDM)

**Discretization:**
$$\frac{\Phi_{i+1,j,k} - 2\Phi_{i,j,k} + \Phi_{i-1,j,k}}{\Delta x^2} + \frac{\Phi_{i,j+1,k} - 2\Phi_{i,j,k} + \Phi_{i,j-1,k}}{\Delta y^2} + \frac{\Phi_{i,j,k+1} - 2\Phi_{i,j,k} + \Phi_{i,j,k-1}}{\Delta z^2} = -\frac{\rho_{i,j,k}}{\varepsilon_0}$$

**Iterative Solution (Gauss-Seidel):**
```python
for iteration in range(max_iterations):
    for i in range(1, nx-1):
        for j in range(1, ny-1):
            for k in range(1, nz-1):
                phi[i,j,k] = (phi[i+1,j,k] + phi[i-1,j,k])/(2*dx**2) + \
                             (phi[i,j+1,k] + phi[i,j-1,k])/(2*dy**2) + \
                             (phi[i,j,k+1] + phi[i,j,k-1])/(2*dz**2) + \
                             rho[i,j,k]/(epsilon_0 * (1/dx**2 + 1/dy**2 + 1/dz**2))
```

**Convergence:** Stop when $|\Phi^{n+1} - \Phi^n| < \epsilon$ (typically $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$)
