## 3. The Binding Mechanism: Neutrino Circulation
In SDT, "binding energy" is not a static potential. It is the **Kinetic Energy of Circulating Neutrinos**.
*   **Why Neutrinos?** Electrons cannot teleport between binding sites. When an electron transitions (e.g., from Neutron to Proton bridge), it emits a **Neutrino** to carry the phase discontinuity.
*   **Closed Circuit:** In a stable nucleus, these neutrinos are not lost. They are reabsorbed by the next transition, forming a permanent **Circulating Flux**.

### 3.1 The Neutrino Population ($N_\nu$)

*   **Binding Channels:** 6 Nucleon Pairs (4 p-n, 1 p-p, 1 n-n).

*   **Total Population:**
    $$ N_\nu = \text{Channels} \times \text{Windings} = 6 \times 3 = 18 $$

The total energy of the circulating neutrinos is derived from the Spation Energy Density ($\rho_s c^2$) scaled by the geometric coupling factors.

$$ B_\alpha = \rho_s c^2 R^3 \times \kappa^4 (1 + \kappa^2) $$

**Geometric Interpretation:**
*   **$\rho_s c^2 R^3$:** The fundamental energy scale of the proton volume ($75.5$ MeV).
*   **$\kappa^4 = 1/4$:** Scaling for the neutrino energy generation.
*   **$(1 + \kappa^2) = 1.5$:** Contribution from both circulating and bound modes.

**Simplified Form:**
$$ B_\alpha = \rho_s c^2 R^3 \times \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{3}{8} \rho_s c^2 R^3 $$

$$ B_\alpha = 0.375 \times 75.5 \text{ MeV} $$
