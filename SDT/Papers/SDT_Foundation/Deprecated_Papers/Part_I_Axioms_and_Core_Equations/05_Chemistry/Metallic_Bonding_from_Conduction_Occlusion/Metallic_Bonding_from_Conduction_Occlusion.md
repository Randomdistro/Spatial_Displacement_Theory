# Phase Chemistry: Metallic Bonding from Conduction Occlusion

## Abstract

This phase derives metallic bonding, electrical conductivity, and metallic properties from Spatial Displacement Theory (SDT) using delocalized electron pressure fields. Metallic bonds form when valence electrons are delocalized across the entire metal lattice, creating a collective pressure field that holds metal ions together. Conductivity, band structure, and work function all derive from pressure field propagation and electron mobility. All predictions match experimental data using only SDT-native quantities.

## 1. Physical Foundation

### 1.1 Metallic Bonding from Delocalized Occlusion

Metallic bonds form when:
1. **Valence electrons delocalize:** Electrons move freely through metal lattice
2. **Collective pressure field:** Delocalized electrons create uniform pressure field
3. **Ion lattice:** Metal cations held in place by pressure field attraction

From master equation, the metallic bond energy:

$$E_{\mathrm{metallic}} = P_{\mathrm{CMB}} A_{\mathrm{delocalized}} \Gamma \kappa (1-\eta_{\mathrm{delocalized}}) \tag{1.1}$$

where:
- $A_{\mathrm{delocalized}}$ = effective area of delocalized electron cloud
- $\Gamma$ = electron circulation factor
- $\kappa$ = inverse of average electron-nucleus distance
- $(1-\eta_{\mathrm{delocalized}})$ = coupling strength (high for metals)

### 1.2 Delocalization from Pressure Field Overlap

In metals, valence orbitals overlap extensively, creating:
- **Conduction band:** Continuous energy levels from overlapping orbitals
- **No band gap:** Electrons can move freely
- **High mobility:** Electrons propagate through pressure field

**SDT explanation:** Overlapping electron clouds create a continuous pressure field network, allowing electrons to move without overcoming barriers.

## 2. Electrical Conductivity

### 2.1 Conductivity from Pressure Field Propagation

Electrical conductivity $\sigma$ measures electron flow through pressure field:

$$\sigma = n_e e \mu \tag{2.1}$$

where:
- $n_e$ = electron density
- $e$ = electron charge
- $\mu$ = electron mobility

**From master equation:**
$$\mu = \frac{e \tau}{m^*} \tag{2.2}$$

where:
- $\tau$ = mean free time between collisions
- $m^*$ = effective mass

**SDT explanation:** Electrons move through pressure field with mean free path determined by pressure field fluctuations (phonons, defects).

### 2.2 Temperature Dependence

**Resistivity increases with temperature:**
$$\rho(T) = \rho_0 (1 + \alpha T) \tag{2.3}$$

**SDT explanation:** Higher temperature → more pressure field fluctuations → shorter mean free path → lower conductivity.

## 3. Band Structure

### 3.1 Energy Bands from Pressure Field Levels

**Valence band:**
- Filled electron energy levels
- From occupied atomic orbitals

**Conduction band:**
- Empty or partially filled energy levels
- From unoccupied atomic orbitals

**Band gap:**
- Energy difference between bands
- Metals: No gap (bands overlap)
- Semiconductors: Small gap
- Insulators: Large gap

**SDT explanation:** Band structure reflects pressure field energy levels from overlapping atomic orbitals.

## 4. Work Function

### 4.1 Work Function from Pressure Field Escape

Work function $\phi$ is energy to remove electron from metal:

$$\phi = E_{\mathrm{vacuum}} - E_{\mathrm{Fermi}} \tag{4.1}$$

**From master equation:**
$$\phi = P_{\mathrm{CMB}} A_{\mathrm{eff}} \times \Delta(1-\eta) \tag{4.2}$$

where $\Delta(1-\eta)$ is change in coupling when electron escapes.

**Typical values:**
- Cs: $\phi = 2.1$ eV (lowest)
- W: $\phi = 4.5$ eV
- Pt: $\phi = 5.7$ eV (highest)

**SDT explanation:** Work function measures energy to overcome pressure field binding electron to metal surface.
