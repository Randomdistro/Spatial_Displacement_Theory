## 2. Electron Magnetic Moment

### 2.1 Classical Current Loop Model

For a toroidal vortex with poloidal radius $r_p$ and toroidal radius $R_t$, the poloidal circulation creates a current loop:

$$I = \frac{e v_p}{2\pi r_p} \tag{2.1}$$

where $v_p$ is the poloidal circulation velocity.

The magnetic moment is:

$$\mu = I \times A = \frac{e v_p}{2\pi r_p} \times \pi r_p^2 = \frac{e v_p r_p}{2} \tag{2.2}$$

### 2.2 Angular Momentum Connection

The angular momentum of the vortex is:

$$L = m_e v_p r_p \tag{2.3}$$

Combining:

$$\mu = \frac{e L}{2 m_e} = \frac{e \hbar/2}{2 m_e} = \frac{e \hbar}{4 m_e} \tag{2.4}$$

This gives the classical value: $g = 2$.

### 2.3 Anomaly from Wake Self-Interaction

The electron g-factor anomaly arises from helical wake self-interaction. The toroidal vortex creates a helical wake pattern that interacts with its own magnetic field, producing a small correction:

$$g_e = 2 + \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} + \cdots = 2.002319304 \tag{2.5}$$

where $\alpha = 1/137.036$ is the fine structure constant.

**Physical interpretation:** The wake interaction creates a small additional contribution to the magnetic moment proportional to the coupling strength $\alpha$.
