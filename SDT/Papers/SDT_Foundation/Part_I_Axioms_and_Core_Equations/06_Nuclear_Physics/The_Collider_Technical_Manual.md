## 3. The Cookbook: Decay Chains and Topology

### 3.1 The Pion ($\pi^\pm$): The Primary Fray
*   **Production:** $p + p \to p + n + \pi^+$.
*   **Geometry:** A simple "Figure-8" loop frayed from the main Proton knot.
*   **Mass:** 140 MeV ($\approx 1/7$ Proton).
*   **Decay Chain (The Unraveling):**
    $$ \pi^+ \to \mu^+ + \nu_\mu $$
    *   **Process:** The Figure-8 loop snaps.
    *   **Output 1 ($\mu^+$):** The loop relaxes into a simpler, heavier circle (The Muon).
    *   **Output 2 ($\nu_\mu$):** The "Snap" releases a chunk of the rope's twist as a **Muon Neutrino**.

### 3.2 The Muon ($\mu^\pm$): The Heavy Electron
*   **Geometry:** A single loop, like an electron, but "Thick" (it retains some of the frayed rope structure).
*   **Mass:** 105 MeV.
*   **Decay Chain:**
    $$ \mu^+ \to e^+ + \nu_e + \bar{\nu}_\mu $$
    *   **Process:** The "Thick" loop sheds its extra strands.
    *   **Output 1 ($e^+$):** The core strand remains as a Positron (Stable Loop).
    *   **Output 2 ($\nu, \bar{\nu}$):** The shed strands fly off as Neutrinos.

### 3.3 The Kaon ($K$): The Twisted Loop
*   **Production:** Associated Production (must be made in pairs).
*   **Geometry:** A loop with a **Torsion Twist** (Strange Quark).
*   **Decay:** It takes longer ($10^{-8}$ s) because the twist locks it. It has to "untwist" before it can snap.

## 4. The Neutrino Budget: Sparks in the Dark
You asked: *"How many neutrinos get trapped?"*

### 4.1 The "Missing Energy"
In a typical collision, we measure the visible debris. We always find **Missing Energy**.
*   **SDT Interpretation:** This is the **Spation Debris**.
*   When you shred a rope, you get dust/fibers.
*   **Neutrinos are the Dust.**

### 4.2 Trapping
*   **Free Neutrinos:** Most escape. They are "Grease" (no charge, no mass). They slip through the detector.
*   **Trapped Neutrinos:** **Zero.** You cannot "trap" a neutrino in a collision. They are pure kinetic geometry. They leave at speed $c$. The only way to "trap" them is to re-absorb them into a nucleus (Inverse Beta Decay), which is incredibly rare ($10^{-40}$ probability).
