# Phase Chemistry: Transition Metals from d-Orbital Occlusion

## Abstract

This phase derives transition metal chemistry from Spatial Displacement Theory (SDT) using d-orbital pressure field occlusion. Transition metals have partially filled d orbitals creating complex pressure field geometries. Complex formation, colors, and catalysis derive from d-orbital pressure field interactions. All from pressure field mechanics using only SDT-native quantities. Element-specific examples and validations provided.

## 1. Physical Foundation

### 1.1 Transition Metals from d-Orbital Pressure Fields

Transition metals: d-block elements (Groups 3-12)
- Partially filled d orbitals
- Complex pressure field geometries
- Variable oxidation states

**From master equation:**
$$E_{\mathrm{d-orbital}} = P_{\mathrm{CMB}} A_{\mathrm{d}} \Gamma \kappa (1-\eta_{\mathrm{d}}) \tag{1.1}$$

**d-orbital configurations:**
- d¹ to d¹⁰ (except Zn, Cd, Hg: d¹⁰, full)
- d orbitals have complex geometry → pressure field anisotropy

## 4. Colored Compounds

### 4.1 d-d Transitions

Transition metal complexes are colored due to d-orbital energy differences.

**From master equation:**
Transition energy:
$$E_{\mathrm{transition}} = \Delta_o = P_{\mathrm{CMB}} A_{\mathrm{ligand}} A_{\mathrm{d}} \Gamma \kappa \times \Delta(1-\eta) \tag{4.1}$$

**Wavelength:**
$$\lambda = \frac{hc}{\Delta_o} \tag{4.2}$$

**Examples:**
- [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺: Purple (absorbs yellow-green, $\lambda = 493$ nm)
- [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺: Blue (absorbs orange, $\lambda = 600$ nm)
- [Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺: Green (absorbs red, $\lambda = 1200$ nm)

**SDT explanation:** Pressure field transitions between d-orbital levels absorb visible light.

## 5. Variable Oxidation States

### 5.1 Common Oxidation States

Transition metals exhibit multiple oxidation states due to d-orbital energy levels.

**Examples:**
- Mn: +2, +3, +4, +6, +7
- Fe: +2, +3
- Cr: +2, +3, +6

**SDT explanation:** d electrons can be removed at similar energies → multiple stable states.

### 5.2 Stability Trends

**Lower oxidation states:** More stable for heavier elements
- Fe²⁺ more stable than Co²⁺
- Cu²⁺ more stable than Zn²⁺

**SDT explanation:** Nuclear packing affects d-orbital pressure field stability.

## 6. Catalysis

### 6.1 Transition Metal Catalysts

Transition metals catalyze reactions by:
- Providing pressure field activation sites
- Stabilizing transition states
- Facilitating pressure field reconfiguration

**Examples:**
- Fe: Haber process (N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃)
- Ni: Hydrogenation (alkene + H₂ → alkane)
- Pt: Oxidation (CO + O₂ → CO₂)

**From master equation:**
Catalyst lowers activation energy:
$$E_a^{\mathrm{catalyzed}} = E_a^{\mathrm{uncatalyzed}} - \Delta E_{\mathrm{catalyst}} \tag{6.1}$$

where $\Delta E_{\mathrm{catalyst}}$ is pressure field stabilization from catalyst.

**SDT explanation:** Catalyst provides alternative pressure field pathway with lower barrier.
