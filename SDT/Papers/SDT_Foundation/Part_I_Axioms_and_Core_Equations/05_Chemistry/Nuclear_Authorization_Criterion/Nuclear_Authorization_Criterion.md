# Nuclear Authorization Criterion for Bond Existence
**SDT Formalization: When Nuclei Permit Chemistry**

## Abstract

This document formalizes the SDT principle that **bonding is electron-geometry-permitted, but nucleus-geometry-authorized**. A bond exists only if the nuclear configuration can sustain the boundary conditions required for the electron architecture to persist. We derive a quantitative, falsifiable criterion using only SDT primitives: occlusion geometry, compression terms, and the kinematic ratio χ.

## 1. The Two-Gate Condition

### Gate A: Electron Solution Exists

For a given nuclear configuration **N**, there exists a bound electronic state (or shared state) $\psi_e$ in the effective potential generated by spation/nuclear boundary conditions.

**Mathematical statement:**
$$\exists \psi_e : \hat{H}_{\text{eff}}[N] \psi_e = E_e \psi_e \quad \text{with} \quad E_e < 0 \tag{1.1}$$

where $\hat{H}_{\text{eff}}[N]$ is the effective Hamiltonian determined by the nuclear geometry **N**.

### Gate B: Nuclear Configuration is Dynamically Admissible

The nucleus must be stable *enough* and *structured enough* to sustain the boundary conditions that make $\psi_e$ real.

**Mathematical statement:**
$$\tau_{\text{nucleus}} \gg \tau_{\text{bond-form}} \quad \text{AND} \quad \text{N is a fixed boundary during bond formation/maintenance} \tag{1.2}$$

## 2. SDT Primitives

### 2.1 Kinematic Ratio χ

The kinematic ratio measures electron stability:
$$\chi = \frac{c}{v_e} \tag{2.1}$$

where:
- $c$ = speed of light
- $v_e$ = electron orbital velocity

**Physical meaning:** Higher χ means lower velocity and weaker binding (more "floppy"). Lower χ means tighter binding.

**Reference values:**
- Hydrogen: $\chi_H = 137.0$ (universal constant of stability)
- Krypton: $\chi_{Kr} = 135.0$ (completed n=4 cube)
- Lithium: $\chi_{Li} \approx 198$ (floppy, reactive)
- Potassium: $\chi_K \approx 242$ (very floppy)

### 2.2 Compression Increment

As protons are added, nuclear compression increases. Define the **compression increment per proton**:
$$\Delta\chi_Z = \chi_{Z+1} - \chi_Z \tag{2.2}$$

**Physical meaning:** Measures how much the electron architecture tightens (or loosens) per added proton.

**Empirical observation:** For stable elements, $\Delta\chi_Z$ is bounded. Excessive compression ($|\Delta\chi_Z|$ too large) indicates nuclear instability.

### 2.3 Occlusion Fraction E

The occlusion fraction measures geometric blocking:
$$E(\hat{\Omega}_k) = \frac{1}{4\pi} \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{core}}} \chi_j(\hat{\Omega}_k) \Omega_{j \to \hat{\Omega}_k} \tag{2.3}$$

where:
- $\chi_j = 1$ if inner site $j$ eclipses the nucleus along direction $\hat{\Omega}_k$, else $0$
- $\Omega_{j \to \hat{\Omega}_k}$ = solid angle contribution

**Averaged occlusion factor:**
$$\Xi_{n\ell} = 1 - \frac{1}{M_{n\ell}} \sum_{k=1}^{M_{n\ell}} E(\hat{\Omega}_k) \tag{2.4}$$

### 2.4 Nuclear Pressure Field

The nuclear spation pressure:
$$P_{\text{nuc}} = 1.65 \times 10^{31} \text{ Pa} \tag{2.5}$$

This pressure generates the effective potential for electron binding.

## 3. The Nuclear Authorization Criterion

### 3.1 Stability Margin

Define the **stability margin** as:
$$\Delta\chi_{\text{stable}} = \chi_{\text{ref}} - \chi_{\text{actual}} \tag{3.1}$$

where $\chi_{\text{ref}} = 137$ (hydrogen baseline).

**Physical meaning:** Positive margin means tighter than hydrogen (stable). Negative margin means looser (potentially unstable).

### 3.2 Compression Gradient Bound

The **compression gradient** must be bounded:
$$\left|\frac{\partial \chi}{\partial Z}\right| = |\Delta\chi_Z| < \chi_{\text{crit}}' \tag{3.2}$$

**Empirical bound:** For stable chemistry, $|\Delta\chi_Z| < 50$ per proton (from Sc→Zn: ~32 units over 10 elements ≈ 3.2 per proton).

### 3.3 The Complete Criterion

**A bond exists if and only if:**

$$\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{(i)} \quad & \tau_{\text{nucleus}} \gg \tau_{\text{bond-form}} \\
\text{(ii)} \quad & \Delta\chi_{\text{stable}} > \Delta\chi_{\min} \\
\text{(iii)} \quad & |\Delta\chi_Z| < \chi_{\text{crit}}' \\
\text{(iv)} \quad & \Xi_{n\ell} > \Xi_{\min}
\end{aligned}
} \tag{3.3}$$

**Where:**
- $\tau_{\text{nucleus}}$ = nuclear lifetime (half-life or stability timescale)
- $\tau_{\text{bond-form}}$ = bond formation timescale ≈ $r_{\text{bond}}/(c\alpha)$ ≈ $10^{-16}$ s
- $\Delta\chi_{\min}$ = minimum stability margin (empirically ≈ -100, allowing up to χ ≈ 237)
- $\chi_{\text{crit}}'$ = maximum compression increment (empirically ≈ 50 per proton)
- $\Xi_{\min}$ = minimum occlusion factor for bound states (empirically ≈ 0.1)

## 4. Quantitative Thresholds

### 4.1 Timescale Requirement

$$\tau_{\text{nucleus}} > 10^3 \times \tau_{\text{bond-form}} \approx 10^{-13} \text{ s} \tag{4.1}$$

**Interpretation:** Nucleus must persist for at least ~1000 bond formation cycles.

**Borderline cases:**
- **Short-lived nuclei:** $\tau_{\text{nucleus}} < 10^{-13}$ s → No chemistry possible
- **Metastable isomers:** $\tau_{\text{nucleus}} \sim 10^{-12}$ to $10^{-6}$ s → Limited chemistry
- **Stable nuclei:** $\tau_{\text{nucleus}} > 10^6$ s → Full chemistry

### 4.2 Stability Margin Threshold

$$\Delta\chi_{\text{stable}} > -100 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \chi < 237 \tag{4.2}$$

**Interpretation:** If $\chi > 237$, the electron is too "floppy" and cannot form stable bonds.

**Empirical support:** Potassium (χ ≈ 242) is highly reactive; elements with χ > 250 are typically unstable.

### 4.3 Compression Increment Threshold

$$|\Delta\chi_Z| < 50 \text{ per proton} \tag{4.3}$$

**Interpretation:** If adding one proton changes χ by more than 50 units, the nuclear geometry cannot support stable electron architecture.

**Empirical support:** Transition metals (Sc→Zn) show $\Delta\chi_Z \approx 3.2$ per proton (stable). Superheavy elements show much larger increments.

### 4.4 Occlusion Factor Threshold

$$\Xi_{n\ell} > 0.1 \tag{4.4}$$

**Interpretation:** At least 10% of the nuclear pressure field must be accessible to electrons for binding.

## 5. Falsifiable Predictions

### 5.1 Superheavy Elements

**Prediction:** Elements with $Z > Z_{\text{crit}}$ where $|\Delta\chi_Z| > 50$ cannot host stable chemistry.

**Test:** Measure χ values for superheavy elements. If $|\Delta\chi_Z| > 50$, predict no stable compounds.

**Expected result:** Periodic table "ends" from nuclear side before electron side.

### 5.2 Nuclear Isomers

**Prediction:** Metastable nuclear isomers with $\tau_{\text{nucleus}} < 10^{-13}$ s cannot form bonds.

**Test:** Attempt to synthesize compounds with short-lived isomers. If $\tau < 10^{-13}$ s, predict no bond formation.

**Expected result:** Isomers with $\tau \sim 10^{-15}$ s show no chemistry; those with $\tau > 10^{-12}$ s may show limited chemistry.

### 5.3 Isotope Effects

**Prediction:** Isotopes with different N/Z ratios will have different $\chi$ values, affecting bond strengths.

**Test:** Measure bond energies for different isotopes. Predict:
$$\Delta E_{\text{bond}} \propto \Delta\chi_{\text{isotope}} \tag{5.1}$$

**Expected result:** Heavy isotopes (more neutrons) should show slightly different bond energies due to nuclear geometry changes.

### 5.4 Technetium (Z=43)

**Prediction:** Technetium has no stable isotopes because its nuclear geometry (combined with 5s²4d⁵ electronic configuration) violates criterion (iii): $|\Delta\chi_Z| > 50$.

**Test:** Measure χ for Tc isotopes. If $|\Delta\chi_Z| > 50$, this explains why no stable isotopes exist.

**Expected result:** Confirms that nuclear geometry, not just electron geometry, determines stability.

## 6. Mathematical Formulation

### 6.1 Complete Authorization Function

Define the **nuclear authorization function**:
$$\mathcal{A}(Z, N, \text{geometry}) = \begin{cases}
1 & \text{if all criteria (i)-(iv) satisfied} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \tag{6.1}$$

**Bond exists if:**
$$\mathcal{A}(Z, N, \text{geometry}) = 1 \quad \text{AND} \quad \exists \psi_e \text{ satisfying Gate A} \tag{6.2}$$

### 6.2 Quantitative Expression

Expanding criterion (3.3) in terms of measurable quantities:

$$\mathcal{A} = \Theta\left(\frac{\tau_{\text{nucleus}}}{\tau_{\text{bond-form}}} - 10^3\right) \times \Theta(\chi_{\max} - \chi) \times \Theta(\chi_{\text{crit}}' - |\Delta\chi_Z|) \times \Theta(\Xi_{n\ell} - \Xi_{\min}) \tag{6.3}$$

where $\Theta(x)$ is the Heaviside step function ($\Theta(x) = 1$ if $x > 0$, else $0$).

### 6.3 Continuous Measure

For quantitative predictions, define the **authorization strength**:
$$S_{\text{auth}} = \min\left(
\frac{\tau_{\text{nucleus}}}{10^3 \tau_{\text{bond-form}}},
\frac{\chi_{\max} - \chi}{100},
\frac{\chi_{\text{crit}}' - |\Delta\chi_Z|}{50},
\frac{\Xi_{n\ell} - \Xi_{\min}}{0.9}
\right) \tag{6.4}$$

**Interpretation:**
- $S_{\text{auth}} > 1$: Full authorization (stable chemistry)
- $0 < S_{\text{auth}} < 1$: Partial authorization (limited chemistry)
- $S_{\text{auth}} < 0$: No authorization (no chemistry)
