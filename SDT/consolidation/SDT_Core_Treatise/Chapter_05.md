## The trefoil–torus model

The proton is modelled as a self-sustaining vortex knot on a fat torus. In knot theory, the Trefoil 3₁ is the simplest nontrivial knot: a closed loop with three crossings. When wrapped on a torus (a doughnut-shaped surface), the knot has two winding numbers: the **poloidal winding** n (times around the tube) and the **toroidal winding** m (times around the hole). For the Trefoil 3₁:

- **n = 3** (poloidal winding)
- **m = 2** (toroidal winding)

These numbers are topological invariants: they characterise the knot regardless of how it is deformed (without cutting). The combination n² − m² = 9 − 4 = 5 is the key invariant that links the proton to the hydrogen scale and the Sun.

## Occlusion and stability: a/R and κ

**Minor-to-major radius ratio: a/R = 1/√2.** On a fat torus, the “minor” radius a is the radius of the tube; the “major” radius R is the distance from the centre of the hole to the centre of the tube. The ratio a/R = 1/√2 ≈ 0.7071 is chosen for stability: it sets the virial limit v_orb/v_esc = 1/√2 (κ ≈ 1/√2). So the geometry of the torus determines the nucleon’s surface velocity: v_surface = cκ with κ = 1/√2 (Chapter 10).

**Internal geometric parameter: κ ≈ 0.694.** The formula κ = π^(1/4) / (n (1+(a/R)²)^(1/4)) with n = 3 and a/R = 1/√2 gives κ ≈ 0.694. This is numerically close to 1/√2 ≈ 0.7071; in nuclear first-principles the virial value κ = 1/√2 is used consistently (F11, F12, D-01). The topology-derived κ ≈ 0.694 and the virial κ = 1/√2 are treated as the same for practical purposes. Both values satisfy the virial condition v_orb/v_esc = 1/√2.

**κ ≈ 1/√2 is the virial stability limit (v_orb/v_esc).** At the nucleon surface, the orbital velocity is v_orb = cκ and the escape velocity is v_esc = √2 cκ (Rule 6). So v_orb/v_esc = 1/√2. The ratio 1/√2 is the virial stability limit: orbits at that ratio are marginally stable. So κ = 1/√2 is both geometric (from torus and trefoil) and dynamical (virial).

## Formula F12: Proton magnetic moment

**Statement:** μ_p = e c R/(2√2); already uses correct κ = 1/√2 (not κ = 1).

**Derivation.** (1) The proton magnetic moment arises from a circulating current at radius R with velocity v = cκ. (2) Magnetic moment scales as μ ∝ e v R (charge × velocity × radius). (3) With κ = 1/√2, v = c/√2, so μ ∝ e c R/√2. The factor 2√2 in the denominator comes from the exact geometry of the trefoil current loop and the κ factor. (4) The result μ_p = e c R/(2√2) matches the measured proton magnetic moment to within known precision (CODATA).

**Why it matters.** The proton magnetic moment is a geometric prediction from trefoil radius R and κ = 1/√2. No separate “anomalous” moment is inserted by hand; the “anomaly” (the deviation from the Dirac value for a point charge) is the κ factor. F12 certifies that the same trefoil structure that gives k_p² = 5 α⁻¹ also gives μ_p. **CRITICAL:** The formula uses κ = 1/√2, not κ = 1. Using κ = 1 would give the wrong magnitude and contradict nuclear first-principles (09_CANONICAL §9; dataset CRITICAL CORRECTION).

**Why a vortex knot?** In SDT, matter displaces the spation medium. A stable “particle” is a self-sustaining displacement structure. **Stability and confinement.** The fat torus with a/R = 1/√2 and κ = 1/√2 is stable under the virial condition. So the trefoil geometry and κ = 1/√2 underpin both the proton’s electromagnetic properties (μ_p) and its role in nuclear binding (D-01).
