## 3. Derivation of Gravitation from Pressure Gradients

We replace the phenomenological mass $M$ and constant $G$ with the rigorous geometric displacement parameter $\beta$.

$$ \Pi_s(r) = \Pi_0 - \frac{\beta \rho_s}{r} \tag{1} $$

where $\beta$ is the **Gravitational Potential Parameter** (units m$^3$/s$^2$):
$$ \beta \equiv \frac{\kappa V_{\text{total}} K_{\text{bulk}}}{4\pi \rho_s} = \frac{\kappa V_{\text{total}} c^2}{4\pi} \tag{2} $$
Here, $\kappa$ is a geometric efficiency factor of the lattice.

### 3.2 The Acceleration Field
A test body immersed in this gradient experiences a net force due to the pressure difference across its diameter.
The pressure gradient is:
$$ \frac{d\Pi_s}{dr} = \frac{\beta \rho_s}{r^2} \tag{3} $$

The force $F$ on a test body of volume $V_{\text{test}}$ is the volume integral of the gradient. The acceleration $a$ is the force per unit density of the displaced medium (Archimedes' principle equivalent):
$$ a(r) = -\frac{1}{\rho_s} \frac{d\Pi_s}{dr} = -\frac{1}{\rho_s} \left( \frac{\beta \rho_s}{r^2} \right) \tag{4} $$

$$ \boxed{ a(r) = -\frac{\beta}{r^2} } \tag{5} $$

This recovers the inverse-square law purely from fluid mechanics. Note that **no gravitational constant G appears**. The parameter $\beta$ completely characterizes the "gravity" of the body. In conventional terms, $\beta \equiv GM$, but $\beta$ is derived from volume and sound speed, not mass.
