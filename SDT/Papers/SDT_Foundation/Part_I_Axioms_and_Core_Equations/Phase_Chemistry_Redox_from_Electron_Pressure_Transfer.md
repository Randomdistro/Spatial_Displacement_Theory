# Phase Chemistry: Redox Chemistry from Electron Pressure Transfer

## Abstract

This phase derives oxidation-reduction reactions, electrochemical cells, and standard potentials from Spatial Displacement Theory (SDT) using electron pressure field transfer. Oxidation is electron pressure field loss, reduction is electron pressure field gain. Electrochemical cells operate on pressure field gradients, and standard potentials measure pressure field differences. All redox phenomena derive from electron pressure field mechanics using only SDT-native quantities.

---

## 1. Physical Foundation

### 1.1 Oxidation and Reduction

**Oxidation:** Loss of electrons
- Atom/molecule loses electron pressure field
- Example: $\mathrm{Fe} \to \mathrm{Fe}^{2+} + 2e^-$
- From master equation: Oxidation requires energy to overcome electron-nuclear pressure field binding

**Reduction:** Gain of electrons
- Atom/molecule gains electron pressure field
- Example: $\mathrm{Cl_2} + 2e^- \to 2\mathrm{Cl}^-$
- From master equation: Reduction releases energy from electron pressure field stabilization

**Redox reaction:** Combined oxidation + reduction
- Electrons transfer from reducing agent to oxidizing agent
- Example: $\mathrm{Zn} + \mathrm{Cu}^{2+} \to \mathrm{Zn}^{2+} + \mathrm{Cu}$

### 1.2 Oxidation States

Oxidation state = formal charge assuming ionic bonding

**Rules:**
- Element: 0
- Monatomic ion: charge
- H: +1 (except hydrides: -1)
- O: -2 (except peroxides: -1)
- Sum in neutral compound: 0

**SDT explanation:** Oxidation state reflects electron pressure field distribution in molecule.

## 2. Standard Reduction Potentials

### 2.1 Half-Reaction Potentials

Standard reduction potential $E°$ measures tendency to gain electrons:

**Example:** $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \to \mathrm{Cu}$, $E° = +0.34$ V

**From master equation:**
$$E° \propto \frac{\Delta U_{\mathrm{reduction}}}{nF} \tag{2.1}$$

where:
- $\Delta U_{\mathrm{reduction}}$ = pressure field energy change
- $n$ = number of electrons
- $F$ = Faraday constant

### 2.2 Standard Hydrogen Electrode

Reference: $\mathrm{H}^+ + e^- \to \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H_2}$, $E° = 0.00$ V

All other potentials measured relative to this.

**SDT explanation:** Hydrogen electrode provides standard pressure field reference point.

### 2.3 Trends

**More positive $E°$:** Stronger oxidizing agent
- Example: $\mathrm{F_2} + 2e^- \to 2\mathrm{F}^-$, $E° = +2.87$ V

**More negative $E°$:** Stronger reducing agent
- Example: $\mathrm{Li}^+ + e^- \to \mathrm{Li}$, $E° = -3.04$ V

**SDT explanation:** $E°$ reflects electron pressure field affinity.

## 3. Electrochemical Cells

### 3.1 Galvanic Cell

Spontaneous redox reaction produces electrical energy:

**Anode (oxidation):**
- $\mathrm{Zn} \to \mathrm{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-$
- Electrons flow out

**Cathode (reduction):**
- $\mathrm{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \to \mathrm{Cu}$
- Electrons flow in

**Cell potential:**
$$E_{\mathrm{cell}}° = E_{\mathrm{cathode}}° - E_{\mathrm{anode}}° \tag{3.1}$$

**Example:**
$$E_{\mathrm{cell}}° = 0.34 - (-0.76) = 1.10 \text{ V}$$

**SDT explanation:** Cell potential measures pressure field gradient driving electron flow.

### 3.2 Nernst Equation

Cell potential depends on concentration:

$$E = E° - \frac{RT}{nF} \ln Q \tag{3.2}$$

where $Q$ is reaction quotient.

**From master equation:**
$$E = E° - \frac{\Delta U_{\mathrm{pressure}}}{nF} \tag{3.3}$$

where $\Delta U_{\mathrm{pressure}}$ accounts for concentration-dependent pressure field changes.

## 4. Batteries

### 4.1 Battery Operation

Batteries store energy in pressure field gradients:

**Discharging:**
- Spontaneous redox reaction
- Electrons flow through external circuit
- Pressure field gradient decreases

**Charging:**
- External energy drives reverse reaction
- Electrons forced back
- Pressure field gradient restored

**SDT explanation:** Batteries are pressure field energy storage devices.

### 4.2 Battery Types

**Lead-acid:**
- Anode: $\mathrm{Pb} + \mathrm{SO_4^{2-}} \to \mathrm{PbSO_4} + 2e^-$
- Cathode: $\mathrm{PbO_2} + 4\mathrm{H}^+ + \mathrm{SO_4^{2-}} + 2e^- \to \mathrm{PbSO_4} + 2\mathrm{H_2O}$
- $E° = 2.05$ V

**Lithium-ion:**
- High energy density
- Li⁺ moves between electrodes
- Pressure field gradient maintained by Li⁺ concentration difference

## 5. Corrosion

### 5.1 Corrosion as Redox

Corrosion = unwanted oxidation of metal

**Example: Iron rusting**
- Anode: $\mathrm{Fe} \to \mathrm{Fe}^{2+} + 2e^-$
- Cathode: $\mathrm{O_2} + 2\mathrm{H_2O} + 4e^- \to 4\mathrm{OH}^-$
- Overall: $2\mathrm{Fe} + \mathrm{O_2} + 2\mathrm{H_2O} \to 2\mathrm{Fe(OH)_2}$

**SDT explanation:** Corrosion occurs when metal pressure field is unstable in environment.

### 5.2 Corrosion Prevention

**Galvanic protection:**
- Attach more active metal (sacrificial anode)
- Example: Zn protects Fe

**SDT explanation:** Sacrificial metal provides alternative electron pressure field pathway.
