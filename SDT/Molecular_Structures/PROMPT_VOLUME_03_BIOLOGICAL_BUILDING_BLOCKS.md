## PART I: VOLUME SCOPE AND ORGANIZATION

### 1.1 Volume Theme

Volume 3 focuses on **biological building blocks** - the fundamental molecules that form the basis of life: amino acids, nucleotides, sugars, lipids, and their simple derivatives. These molecules are produced by living organisms and form the structural and functional foundation of all biological systems.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Produced by living organisms
- Form larger biological structures (proteins, DNA, RNA, polysaccharides)
- Contain C, H, O, N, P, S
- Typically chiral (handedness from nuclear geometry)
- Form hydrogen bonds (nuclear force networks)
- Water-soluble or membrane-forming

### 1.2 Classification Criteria

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Amino acids (20 standard + variants)
- Nucleotides (DNA and RNA bases + sugars + phosphates)
- Simple sugars (monosaccharides, disaccharides)
- Fatty acids and simple lipids
- Biological cofactors and vitamins
- Simple biological polymers (dipeptides, dinucleotides, etc.)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Complex proteins (>10 amino acids)
- Complex nucleic acids (>10 nucleotides)
- Complex polysaccharides (>10 sugar units)
- Complex lipids (reserved for later volumes)

### 3.2 Hydrogen Bonding Analysis (NUCLEUS-DRIVEN)

**CRITICAL:** Hydrogen bonds are NOT separate bonds - they are extended nuclear force networks.

**For hydrogen bonds, provide:**

**A. Nuclear Force Network:**
- Donor nucleus (e.g., N, O)
- H nucleus (single proton)
- Acceptor nucleus (e.g., O, N)
- Nuclear force chain: Donor → H → Acceptor

**B. Bond Length:**
- Donor-H distance (covalent, ~100 pm)
- H-Acceptor distance (hydrogen bond, ~180 pm)
- Total Donor-Acceptor distance (~280 pm)

**C. Bond Energy:**
- Depth of extended nuclear force well
- Typically 10-40 kJ/mol
- Much weaker than covalent (single nuclear well vs extended network)

**D. Network Formation:**
- Multiple hydrogen bonds form nuclear force networks
- DNA base pairing: Nuclear force networks
- Protein folding: Nuclear force networks
- NOT electron sharing - nuclear force chains
