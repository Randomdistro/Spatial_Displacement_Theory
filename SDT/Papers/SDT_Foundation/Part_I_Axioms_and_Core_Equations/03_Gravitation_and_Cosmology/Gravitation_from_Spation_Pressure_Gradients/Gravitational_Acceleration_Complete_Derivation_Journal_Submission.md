The SDT formulation, however, requires no empirically-fitted gravitational constant and treats 'mass' as an emergent property of boundary pressure dynamics rather than an intrinsic property of matter. We present falsifiable predictions that distinguish SDT from General Relativity and discuss implications for gravitational physics.

## 4. Gravitational Acceleration from Pressure Screening

### 4.1 The Pressure Field Around a Body
Consider a spherical body of N nucleons with effective radius R. At distance r >> R from the center, the body creates a pressure perturbation in the surrounding spation:

```
Πs(r) = Π0 − (κ Vtotal Kbulk)/(4πr)                    (3)
```

where Π0 is ambient pressure, κ is a geometric efficiency factor from the lattice structure, Vtotal = N·Vn is total displacement volume, and Kbulk = ρsc² is spation bulk modulus.

Define the gravitational potential parameter β:

```
β ≡ (κ Vtotal c²)/(4π)                    (4)
```

with units [β] = m³/s². The pressure field becomes:

```
Πs(r) = Π0 − β ρs/r                    (5)
```

### 4.2 Force from Pressure Imbalance
A test body at distance r experiences different pressures on its near and far sides:

```
Πnear = Πs(r − Rtest),     Πfar = Πs(r + Rtest)
```

For Rtest << r, the pressure differential across the test body is:

```

```

This produces a net force pushing the test body toward the central body. The acceleration experienced is:

```
a(r) = −(1/ρs) dΠs/dr = −β/r²                    (6)
```

This is the fundamental gravitational acceleration formula in SDT.
