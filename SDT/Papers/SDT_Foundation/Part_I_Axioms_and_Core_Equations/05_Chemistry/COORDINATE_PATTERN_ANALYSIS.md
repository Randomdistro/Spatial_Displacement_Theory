## Pattern Analysis

### Polar Angles (θ)

**Alternating pattern:**
- 120°, 60°, 120°, 60°, 120°, 60°, 120°

**What this means:**
- 120° = 60° below north pole (or 60° above south pole)
- 60° = 30° below north pole (or 30° above equator)
- These alternate: one point closer to north, one closer to equator

### Azimuthal Angles (φ)

**Pattern:**
- 1°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240°, 300°, 360° (or 0°)

**What this means:**
- Roughly 60° increments: 0°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240°, 300°, 360°
- Plus one at 1° (very close to 0°)

## What This Describes: Confirmed

### Icosahedral Arrangement - Sphere Centers

**Confirmed:** These are the **centers** of spheres in an icosahedral arrangement.

**Shell structure:**
- **Shell 0 (Center):** r₀ = 0 (single central sphere)
- **Shell 1:** r₁ = 2Rₛ = D (where Rₛ is sphere radius, D is diameter)
- **Shell k:** rₖ = k·D = 2kRₛ

**For our case:**
- r = 10 (sphere radius)
- Shell 1: r₁ = 2r = 20
- The 7 coordinates are **centers** of 7 of the 12 icosahedral vertices
- All at radius 2r = 20

**The pattern:**
- Alternating polar angles: 120°, 60°, 120°, 60°, 120°, 60°, 120°
- Azimuthal angles: 1°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240°, 300°, 360°
- Creates the icosahedral structure
- These are sphere **centers**, not the spheres themselves

### Spherical Coordinate Convention

**Mathematics convention (this work):**
- (r, θ, φ) where:
  - r = radial distance
  - θ = azimuthal angle (0 to 2π) - angle in xy-plane
  - φ = polar/zenith angle (0 to π) - angle from z-axis

**Note:** Physics convention reverses θ and φ, but we're using mathematics convention here.

**The coordinates:**
- r = 2r = 20 (radial distance)
- θ = azimuthal angles (1°, 60°, 120°, etc.)
- φ = polar angles (120°, 60°, 120°, etc.)
