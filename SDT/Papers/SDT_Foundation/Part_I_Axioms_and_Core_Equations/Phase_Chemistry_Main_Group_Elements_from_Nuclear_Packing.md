# Phase Chemistry: Main Group Elements from Nuclear Packing

## Abstract

This phase derives main group element chemistry (Groups 1-2, 13-18) from Spatial Displacement Theory (SDT) using nuclear packing geometry. Element properties and trends derive from nuclear size, charge, and electron cloud occlusion. All from nuclear packing master equation. Element-specific examples and validations provided for each group.

## 1. Physical Foundation

### 1.1 Main Groups from Nuclear Packing

Main group elements: s and p blocks
- Properties determined by nuclear packing + valence electrons
- Trends follow periodic table (see Phase Chemistry Periodic Table)

**From master equation:**
All properties derive from $\dot{E} = P_{\infty} A_{\mathrm{eff}} \Gamma \kappa (1-\eta)$

**Nuclear packing parameters:**
- Nuclear radius: $R_{\mathrm{nuc}} = r_0 A^{1/3}$ where $r_0 = 1.2 \times 10^{-15}$ m
- Nuclear charge: $Z$ (number of protons)
- Electron configuration: Determines valence electron pressure fields

## 2. Group 1: Alkali Metals

### 2.1 Properties

- Configuration: ns¹
- Low ionization energy
- Form +1 cations
- Increasing reactivity down group

**SDT explanation:** Single valence electron, weak nuclear coupling → easy to remove.

### 2.2 Examples

| Element | Z | $I_1$ (kJ/mol) | $r$ (pm) | Reactivity |
|---------|---|----------------|----------|------------|
| Li | 3 | 520 | 152 | Moderate |
| Na | 11 | 496 | 186 | High |
| K | 19 | 419 | 227 | Very high |
| Rb | 37 | 403 | 248 | Very high |
| Cs | 55 | 376 | 265 | Extreme |

**SDT explanation:** Increasing $n$ → weaker nuclear pressure field coupling → lower $I_1$ → higher reactivity.

### 2.3 Characteristic Reactions

**With water:**
$$\mathrm{M} + \mathrm{H_2O} \to \mathrm{MOH} + \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{H_2}$$

**With halogens:**
$$2\mathrm{M} + \mathrm{X_2} \to 2\mathrm{MX}$$

**SDT explanation:** Low $I_1$ → easy electron donation → strong reducing agents.

## 3. Group 2: Alkaline Earth Metals

### 3.1 Properties

- Configuration: ns²
- Form +2 cations
- Less reactive than Group 1
- Higher melting points

**SDT explanation:** Two valence electrons → stronger nuclear coupling → higher $I_1$ → less reactive.

### 3.2 Examples

| Element | Z | $I_1$ (kJ/mol) | $I_2$ (kJ/mol) | $r$ (pm) |
|---------|---|----------------|----------------|----------|
| Be | 4 | 900 | 1757 | 112 |
| Mg | 12 | 738 | 1451 | 160 |
| Ca | 20 | 590 | 1145 | 197 |
| Sr | 38 | 550 | 1064 | 215 |
| Ba | 56 | 503 | 965 | 222 |

**SDT explanation:** $I_2$ much higher than $I_1$ due to core electron removal.

## 8. Group 17: Halogens

### 8.1 Properties

- Configuration: ns²np⁵
- All nonmetals
- Form -1, +1, +3, +5, +7 oxidation states
- Strong oxidizing agents

**SDT explanation:** Seven valence electrons → strong tendency to gain one electron.

### 8.2 Examples

| Element | Z | $EA$ (kJ/mol) | $r$ (pm) | Reactivity |
|---------|---|---------------|----------|------------|
| F | 9 | 328 | 72 | Extreme |
| Cl | 17 | 349 | 99 | Very high |
| Br | 35 | 325 | 114 | High |
| I | 53 | 295 | 133 | Moderate |
| At | 85 | ~270 | ~150 | Low (radioactive) |

**SDT explanation:** High electron affinity → strong oxidizing agents.

## 9. Group 18: Noble Gases

### 9.1 Properties

- Configuration: ns²np⁶ (closed shell)
- All gases
- Very low reactivity
- Form compounds only with strongest oxidizers

**SDT explanation:** Closed shell → stable pressure field configuration → very high $I_1$ → inert.

### 9.2 Examples

| Element | Z | $I_1$ (kJ/mol) | Compounds |
|---------|---|----------------|-----------|
| He | 2 | 2372 | None (stable) |
| Ne | 10 | 2081 | None (stable) |
| Ar | 18 | 1521 | Very few |
| Kr | 36 | 1351 | Some (KrF₂) |
| Xe | 54 | 1170 | Many (XeF₂, XeF₄, XeF₆) |
| Rn | 86 | 1037 | Some (radioactive) |

**SDT explanation:** Decreasing $I_1$ down group → Xe and Rn can form compounds.
