$$r_h = \frac{R}{\vartheta^2} \tag{2.1}$$

where $R$ is the body radius and $\vartheta = c/v_{\text{surface}}$ is the velocity ratio (see Coulomb Force, §3.1).

**Proof:**

From the SDT orbital velocity law (see Coulomb Force, §3.1.7):
$$v(r) = \frac{c}{\vartheta} \sqrt{\frac{R}{r}} \tag{2.1b}$$

At the c-boundary $r = r_c$:
$$c = \frac{c}{\vartheta} \sqrt{\frac{R}{r_c}} \tag{2.1c}$$

Solving for $r_c$:
$$1 = \frac{1}{\vartheta} \sqrt{\frac{R}{r_c}} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \vartheta = \sqrt{\frac{R}{r_c}} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \vartheta^2 = \frac{R}{r_c} \quad \Rightarrow \quad r_c = \frac{R}{\vartheta^2} \tag{2.1d}$$

**Note:** SDT predicts $r_h = R/\vartheta^2$, while GR predicts $r_s = 2GM/c^2$. The factor of 2 difference requires further investigation, but both predict similar scales for black holes.
