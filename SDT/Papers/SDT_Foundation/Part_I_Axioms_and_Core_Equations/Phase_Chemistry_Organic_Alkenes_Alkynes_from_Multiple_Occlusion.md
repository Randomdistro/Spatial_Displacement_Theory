# Phase Chemistry: Organic Alkenes and Alkynes from Multiple Occlusion

## Abstract

This phase derives alkene and alkyne structure, stereochemistry, and addition reactions from Spatial Displacement Theory (SDT) using multiple electron pair occlusion. Alkenes have double bonds (σ + π), alkynes have triple bonds (σ + 2π). Stereochemistry (cis/trans, E/Z) derives from pressure field geometry. Addition reactions involve pressure field reconfiguration. All from pressure field mechanics.

---

## 1. Physical Foundation

### 1.1 Alkenes from Double Bond Occlusion

Alkenes: $\mathrm{C_nH_{2n}}$ (one C=C bond)

**Double bond:**
- σ bond: Head-on overlap
- π bond: Sideways overlap
- Bond length: ~134 pm (shorter than single: 154 pm)

**From master equation:**
$$E_{\pi} = P_{\mathrm{CMB}} A_{\pi} \Gamma \kappa (1-\eta_{\pi}) \tag{1.1}$$

where $A_{\pi} < A_{\sigma}$ (weaker occlusion).

## 2. Stereochemistry

### 2.1 Cis/Trans Isomerism

**Cis:** Same side (higher energy)
- Pressure field repulsion between substituents

**Trans:** Opposite sides (lower energy)
- Minimized pressure field repulsion

**SDT explanation:** Geometry minimizes pressure field energy.

### 2.2 E/Z Nomenclature

E (entgegen): Opposite sides
Z (zusammen): Same side

---

## 3. Alkynes

### 3.1 Triple Bonds

Alkynes: $\mathrm{C_nH_{2n-2}}$ (one C≡C bond)

**Triple bond:**
- One σ + two π bonds
- Bond length: ~120 pm
- Linear geometry

## 4. Addition Reactions

### 4.1 Electrophilic Addition

Example: $\mathrm{H_2C=CH_2} + \mathrm{HBr} \to \mathrm{CH_3CH_2Br}$

**Mechanism:**
1. Electrophile attacks π bond
2. Carbocation intermediate
3. Nucleophile adds

**SDT explanation:** Pressure field reconfiguration through transition states.
