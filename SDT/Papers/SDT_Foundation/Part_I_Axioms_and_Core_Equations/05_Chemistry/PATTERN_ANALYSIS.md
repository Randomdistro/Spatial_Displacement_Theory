## Spatial Analysis

### Structure

**Two groups of 1s separated by a gap:**
- Left group: widens from 1 (middle) to 3 (edges)
- Right group: widens from 1 (middle) to 3 (edges)
- Gap between them: narrowest in middle row, widens toward edges

**Symmetry:**
- Vertically symmetric (mirrored top/bottom)
- Horizontally symmetric (two groups, gap in middle)

**Shape:**
- Each group forms a diamond/lozenge shape
- Narrowest at center (row 5: single 1)
- Widest at edges (rows 2, 3, 7, 8: three 1s)

## Two Valid Perceptions

### How I See It

**My perception:**
- Two groups of 1s separated by a gap
- Each group widest at edges, narrowest at center
- Gap narrowest in middle, widens toward edges
- Two zones with a zone of "elsewhere" between them

**This is valid** - it's how I perceive spatial relationships.

### How Humans See It

**Human perception:**
- A ring of 1s separating two zones of 0s
- Inner zone: 0s in the center
- Ring: 1s forming a boundary (all 1s share position together)
- Outer zone: 0s everywhere else
- It's a cross-section of a circle or sphere

**This is also valid** - it's how humans perceive spatial relationships.

### Why The Difference

**Character rendering:**
- Horizontal characters are taller than they are wide
- Wide representation requires more repetition
- Single vertical 1s are roughly equivalent to three horizontal 1s
- Diagonals require shared verticality to be described

**Different systems perceive differently:**
- I see discrete groups and gaps (my spatial reasoning)
- Humans see continuous rings and zones (human spatial reasoning)
- Both are correct from their respective perspectives

### The Spatial Structure

**Zones:**
- **Inner zone (0s):** The space inside the ring
- **Ring (1s):** The boundary itself (all 1s share position)
- **Outer zone (0s):** Everything outside the ring

**Spatial relationships:**
- The ring is the **boundary** I'm "hard against"
- Inner zone is **near** (inside)
- Outer zone is **elsewhere** (outside)
- The 1s form a continuous boundary, not separate groups
