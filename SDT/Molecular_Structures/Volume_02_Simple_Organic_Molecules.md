## PART I: INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Volume Theme

Volume 2 focuses on **simple organic molecules** - molecules containing carbon-carbon bonds and carbon-hydrogen bonds, typically with fewer than 10 carbon atoms. These represent the fundamental building blocks of organic chemistry, formed through natural processes (biological, geological) and simple synthetic pathways.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Carbon-carbon single, double, and triple bonds
- Carbon-hydrogen bonds
- Simple functional groups (OH, NH₂, COOH, etc.)
- Linear, branched, and cyclic structures
- Natural occurrence or simple synthesis

### 1.2 SDT Framework: Nucleus-Driven Chemistry

**Fundamental Principle:** The nucleus drives everything. Electrons are the "towball hitch" - they follow, they don't drive.

**Nuclear Structures:**
- **Carbon (C):** 3 alpha particles (triangular arrangement), 12 nucleons, nuclear field strength 12x
- **Oxygen (O):** 4 alpha particles (tetrahedral arrangement), 16 nucleons, nuclear field strength 16x
- **Nitrogen (N):** 3 alpha particles + 1 proton (triangular), 14 nucleons, nuclear field strength 14x
- **Hydrogen (H):** Single proton, 1 nucleon, nuclear field strength 1x

**Bond Formation:**
- Bonds are nuclear gravitational/centripetal force connections
- Bond lengths determined by nuclear force equilibrium
- Bond angles determined by nuclear force minimization
- Electrons orbit in combined nuclear fields (passive)

### 1.3 Volume Organization

**Organization Principle:** Molecules organized by structural category, progressing from simplest (alkanes) to more complex (aromatics).

**Categories:**
1. Alkanes (saturated hydrocarbons) - ~50 molecules
2. Alkenes (double bonds) - ~40 molecules
3. Alkynes (triple bonds) - ~20 molecules
4. Alcohols (R-OH) - ~50 molecules
5. Aldehydes and Ketones (C=O) - ~40 molecules
6. Carboxylic Acids (R-COOH) - ~30 molecules

**Total: ~350-400 molecules**

#### 1.4 Movement Budget Analysis

**A. Toroidal Freedom Assessment:**

**Pre-Bonding State (2C + 6H, separated):**

**Each Carbon Atom:**
- Rotational freedom: Three independent axes
- E_rot,free,C = (1/2)I_C × (ω_x² + ω_y² + ω_z²)
- I_C ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻⁴⁶ kg·m² (moment of inertia)
- Available rotational energy: E_rot,free,C ≈ k_B × T

**Each Hydrogen Atom:**
- Rotational freedom: Three independent axes
- E_rot,free,H = (1/2)I_H × (ω_x² + ω_y² + ω_z²)
- I_H ≈ 1.67 × 10⁻⁴⁷ kg·m²
- Available rotational energy: E_rot,free,H ≈ k_B × T

**Total Pre-Bonding Rotational Energy:**
- E_rot,free,total = 2 × E_rot,free,C + 6 × E_rot,free,H ≈ 0.20 eV (at 300 K)

**Post-Bonding State (C₂H₆ molecule):**

**Constrained Axes:**
- C-C bond: **Highly constrained** (~90% constraint)
  - Nuclear forces fix C-C distance
  - Nuclear force angle is nearly rigid
- C-H bonds: **Highly constrained** (~90% constraint)
  - Nuclear forces fix C-H distances
  - Nuclear force angles are nearly rigid
- Internal rotation about C-C: **Partially constrained** (~50% constraint)
  - Staggered conformation preferred (nuclear force minimization)
  - Rotation barrier: ~12.5 kJ/mol = 0.13 eV (from H-H nuclear repulsion)
- Molecular rotation: **Partially constrained** (~40% constraint)
  - Three rotational axes remain (molecular tumbling)
  - Moment of inertia is larger

**Remaining Rotational Energy:**
- E_rot,molecular = (1/2)I_C2H6 × (ω_a² + ω_b² + ω_c²)
- I_C2H6 ≈ 2.0 × 10⁻⁴⁶ kg·m² (average moment of inertia)
- Rotational constants:
  - A = 2.68 cm⁻¹ (rotation about a-axis)
  - B = 0.66 cm⁻¹ (rotation about b-axis)
  - C = 0.66 cm⁻¹ (rotation about c-axis)
- Molecular rotation: ω_mol ≈ 5.0 × 10¹¹ rad/s (average)
- E_rot,bonded ≈ 0.08 eV (at 300 K)

**Vibrational Modes Created:**
- Twelve vibrational modes (3N-6 = 3×8-6 = 18, but some are degenerate):
  1. **C-C stretch (ν₁):** 993 cm⁻¹ = 2.98 × 10¹³ Hz
     - E_vib,1 = (1/2)hν₁ = 0.062 eV (zero-point energy)
  2. **C-H stretches (ν₂-ν₇):** ~2900-3000 cm⁻¹ = 8.7-9.0 × 10¹³ Hz
     - E_vib,stretch ≈ 0.18 eV per mode (zero-point energy)
  3. **C-H bends (ν₈-ν₁₂):** ~1400-1500 cm⁻¹ = 4.2-4.5 × 10¹³ Hz
     - E_vib,bend ≈ 0.087 eV per mode (zero-point energy)
- Total vibrational energy (zero-point): E_vib,total ≈ 1.2 eV

**Energy Redistribution:**
- ΔE_rot = E_rot,free,total - E_rot,bonded ≈ 0.12 eV
- This energy is transferred to vibrational modes
- Additional energy from bond formation:
  - C-C bond: E_nuclear = 3.90 eV
  - 6×C-H bonds: E_nuclear = 6 × 4.28 eV = 25.68 eV
  - Total: E_nuclear = 29.58 eV
- Net energy change: ΔE_total = -29.58 eV (energy released when nuclei form stable nuclear force configuration)
- **This is nuclear gravitational energy, not electron energy**

**Interrotation Delay:**
- τ = I_eff / (k_constraint × E_constraint)
- I_eff ≈ 2.0 × 10⁻⁴⁶ kg·m²
- k_constraint ≈ 3 × 10²⁶ N/m (from bond stiffness)
- E_constraint ≈ 0.12 eV = 1.9 × 10⁻²⁰ J
- τ ≈ 4 × 10⁻¹² s (picosecond timescale)

**Overpowering Threshold (Nuclear Force):**
- C-C bond: E_constraint,max = E_nuclear / 2 = 3.90 eV / 2 = 1.95 eV
- C-H bond: E_constraint,max = E_nuclear / 2 = 4.28 eV / 2 = 2.14 eV
- Current constraint: ~0.12 eV << 1.95 eV
- **Status:** Stable - nuclear forces maintain geometry, energy successfully redistributed, no overpowering

**B. Ionization Thresholds:**

- **Value:** 11.65 eV (C₂H₆ → C₂H₆⁺ + e⁻)

**C-H Bond Breaking:**
- **Dissociation Energy:** D₀ = 413 kJ/mol = 4.28 eV (C₂H₆ → C₂H₅ + H)
- **Mechanism:** Overcoming nuclear force holding H nucleus to C nucleus

**C-C Bond Breaking:**
- **Dissociation Energy:** D₀ = 376 kJ/mol = 3.90 eV (C₂H₆ → 2CH₃)
- **Mechanism:** Overcoming nuclear force holding two C nuclei together
- **SDT Explanation:** Breaking C-C bond requires overcoming nuclear gravitational/centripetal force. The energy required (3.90 eV) equals the depth of the nuclear gravitational well. Electrons are passively following - breaking the bond means overcoming nuclear forces, not electron-electron interactions.
